From the client record

Why this tender exists.

Between 2035 and 2050 the Foundation Society proved that people could live off Earth, and then spent its weight on the Moon: Beacon, Acropolis, the Twin Archers, Lodestar. The road exists. The Collective's members judged that the next useful risk was outward, not another lunar programme.

Ymir, in Mars orbit since 2055, is the foothold. Three months into its survey, the Vision Rover reported organic byproducts under the soil of Gusev Crater. The Collective has tendered LEAP as a rapidly deployable settlement to investigate, and to move when a better patch of ground appears.

Gusev was the right kind of place to land a search even before this rover. Spirit's work there, published in 2004, showed a crater with a long aqueous history. The client's new measurements are the reason for the hurry. The old geology is the reason the hurry is pointed at this crater.

The work on the ground

A hub for the search, not a second rover.

Ten TRAIL rovers come with the contract. Eight are expected to be working at once. Kepler does not redesign them. We design the place that tasks them, takes their samples, mends them, and sends them out again without carrying Earth into the soil they are sampling.

The client has already fixed the hard parts of that bay. It is unpressurised and used for more than one job. It can change a component up to 700 mm on a side, reach the underside, work sterile, and put 25 kWh into a rover in thirty minutes. A TRAIL is 2.94 m long, 715 kg, and carries a 25 kWh battery that is meant to last a week of ordinary driving. The bay has to respect that machine, including a ground clearance of 650 mm and an arm that reaches 1.82 m.

The settlement

What the client has already bound us to.

People

Twenty, in turns of three months.

Permanent staff, not a visiting day shift. They run the science and the base. A normal delivery is no bigger than 5 m³, and the settlement has to keep going for three months with nothing arriving at all. Expeditions leave the base for up to twelve hours, under a written rule for safety, isolation, and contamination.

Volume

1,500 cubic metres, pressurised, and shared.

Most rooms do more than one job. Specialist benches fit equipment no larger than 1 m³. Reduced gravity and the unpressurised bay are part of how the work gets done, not inconveniences to be designed out. The volume cap is a business limit as much as a structural one: every cubic metre we spend on comfort is a cubic metre we do not have for a spare.

The move

Two containers. A geodesic dome. Seventy-two hours.

The pressure shell is a rigid geodesic dome. Triangular panels share their edges, stand on the sand, and pack flat. Everything except the research equipment and the rovers fits into two CASSSCs. Internal space of each container is 9 m by 4.5 m by 4.5 m. Assembly and disassembly each take no more than seventy-two hours, with hand tools and ordinary carry loads. The client refused fabric. A dome of stiff panels can be taken apart and raised again without becoming a balloon. The ship's design is outside this contract. The packed load is not.

The ground

No Earth life out. Little scar left behind.

The client has asked for both passive features and active systems, and for an emergency cleanup if organisms are released. Footings have to take a slope of 5° and local steps of 500 mm, and spread the weight on sand. A base that cuts a trench it cannot fill has failed the brief even if the science that week was good.

Science

The search, and two things it can carry.

The primary objective is the client's: is there life in this ground, and can we ask that again at the next site? The two objectives below are Kepler's, they are low readiness, and either of them comes off the manifest if volume, power, or the seventy-two hours are threatened. They do not sit in the sample laboratory.

01

An inheritance pallet.

A small exterior set of sensors, inside the range Angstrom already sells: temperature, pressure, light, dust. The same measurements, taken the same way, at every site LEAP occupies.

The science return is a record the next mission can trust before it commits a footing or a power system. It lives outside the pressurised volume and packs with the shell. If it fails in the dust, the life search does not.

02

A passive seismic watch.

A listener coupled to the ground for one stay, then lifted and packed. It is there to hear local activity and to take a first look at layering under that patch of Gusev.

The return is a better choice of the next site, and a warning about ground that will not take a footing. The immature part is the coupling: sand, and a slope up to 5°, may refuse it. It has no path into the samples. A silent watch is a failed extra, not a failed mission.

Both are proposed extras. Primary science remains the detection work the rovers and the laboratory are there to do.
