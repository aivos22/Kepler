Delivery

From the award to the tenth year.

The client has asked for a schedule through build, transport and landing, the maintenance that follows, the unknowns that can unseat it, and the places where the crew's safety and the science have to be held together.

Schedule

Four dates the rest of the work hangs on.

Company baseline. Dates below are Kepler's programme. Interface numbers close with engineering before anything is treated as final.

  1. 2056

    Award and a frozen baseline.

    Contract award is 7 November 2056. In the first quarter Kepler freezes the four interfaces the schedule actually depends on: pressurised volume, the two-container pack, the power allowed during a move as well as during science, and the contamination boundary. Nothing is built against a number the departments have not signed.

  2. 2057

    Qualify the shell, then qualify the pack.

    The pressure shell, the airlocks, and the packing plan are tested against the client's conditions: a rigid structure, hand tools, a slope of 5°, steps of 500 mm, and the clock. A full pack into two CASSSCs is a gate. If the rehearsal misses seventy-two hours, the design changes before a container is shipped. Manufacture does not start on a pack that has only been drawn.

  3. 2058

    Build, pack again, hand over a labelled load.

    Manufacture, fit-out, and a second timed pack. Transport is the client's vehicle and sits outside this contract. Kepler's deliverable is a labelled load, the things needed first standing at the door of each container, and a crew who have already built the base on a rehearsal range. In-situ delivery and first assembly are no later than 7 November 2058.

  4. 2068

    Ten years of stays, and the moves between them.

    The first science rotation opens the mission. Twenty people, three months at a time. The base moves when a new site has earned it: seventy-two hours to strip, seventy-two to build. The tenth year, not the first, is the year the power and the spares still have to work.

Upkeep

Maintenance inside limits the client has already set.

Crew time

The duty rotation does the work. No second team arrives to take the base apart. A move that needs more people than are on the ground is a failed design.

Tools

Hand tools, and carry loads a suited person can make. If a task wants a machine the manifest does not have, the task is redesigned.

Volume

Spares required for a move are packed before the base ever needs them. A delivery of up to 5 m³ may restock filters, cleanup material, and science consumables. It is not allowed to be the item a relocation is waiting on.

Energy

Assembly and strip are their own load, separate from science. Operations owns the wattage. This page will not publish a figure the power system has not signed.

Each rotation

Inspect the pressure boundary, the airlocks, the sample store, and the rover interface. Confirm the cleanup kit for one immediate release is at the airlock, and replace what the last stay used.

Each move

A deeper inspection while the shell is open: seals, footings, the pack list against what is actually in the containers. Lessons from a late step are written into the next pack plan before the following site.

Unknowns

What we cannot yet close, and what it costs.

The client has asked for the sensitivities that can decide whether LEAP arrives, works, and stays clean. These are the ones that move the programme. Design maturity is on the list because pretending otherwise is how mass grows in secret.

Dust and soiling

SourceThe Martian atmosphere. Any solar array, including Lightweave's rolled panels, loses output as dust settles and as the Sun moves off the face.

ImpactThe power left at the end of a stay, which is also the power left to take the base apart. A margin that only works on a clear morning is not a margin.

Ground we have not stood on

SourceGusev itself, and every later site. The client allows slopes to 5° and steps to 500 mm. The next patch is not this patch.

ImpactFootings, level, and the seventy-two hours. A pack rehearsed on flat ground can miss the clock on sand.

A release of Earth life

SourceCrew, drills, waste, a damaged seal, a sample brought inside and then a mistake on the way out.

ImpactThe question LEAP exists to ask. One uncontained release can end the science at that site. Cleanup is a rehearsal, not a paragraph.

The seventy-two-hour day

SourceHand assembly in suits, on a slope, with the crew who also have to live there. Suit dexterity for this work is still a design item.

ImpactEvery hour over the cap is an hour the search is stopped. The packing trial is how this is found before Mars finds it.

Power that ages

SourceIf a Kobold generator is on the manifest, Fission Frontiers states a fall of 0.8% each year from assembly. The heat is real from day one. The electrical output is 250 W at the start, and less after that.

ImpactThe tenth year has to be calculated on the aged figure. A budget written for week one will not heat, light, or move the base in 2068.

A baseline that is still moving

SourceThis proposal is the baseline, not the as-built. Several interfaces are still with the other departments today.

ImpactRework, mass growth, and a pack that no longer closes inside two containers. The control is the freeze in the first quarter, and a packing trial that is allowed to fail in 2057.

Losing the samples

SourceFire, a shared atmosphere, and a very small volume. The client has asked for the sample store to be protected when something burns.

ImpactThe primary science. A base that saves the crew and cooks the samples has answered the wrong brief.

Three months with no ship

SourceThe unsupported window is in the brief because isolation breaks crews, and a broken crew breaks the work.

ImpactWellbeing is a mission system with a mass and a volume. If it is funded from whatever space is left, it will be the first thing cut, and the first thing we regret.

Balances

Three places we refused to pretend there was no cost.

01

Physical

A shell that can be rebuilt, not a shell that packs small.

The client has required rigid pressure structure, so a base that is taken apart and put back is not trusting fabric. Kepler's shell is a geodesic dome of stiff triangular panels. They pack flat, and they cost more mass than a skin would. An inflatable would move faster and fail more quietly, which on a ten-year campaign of moves is the wrong saving. Crew safety and the chance of doing the move more than once sit on the same dome.

02

Procedural, with a physical kit

Reach the work, and do not bring Earth with us.

Rovers are serviced outside. People walk for up to twelve hours. That is the mission, and it is also the likely way Earth microorganisms leave the airlock. Kepler's position is that sterile procedure, a cleanup drill, and a slower science day are the price of an answer the Collective can publish. A faster sample is worthless if the sample is ours. The kit for one immediate cleanup is staged at the airlock. The discipline is practised each rotation.

03

Physical and procedural

Stay the three months, and still be able to leave.

The volume is already tight, the resupply is small, and the crew has to remain fit to work. Kepler will spend pressurised room on sleep, exercise, and a shared table, and will keep the spares that a move depends on inside the containers rather than in the living space. Comfort that prevents a move is not comfort. A spare policy that leaves people unable to work the next month is not a saving. Both are checked again at the packing trial, against the 1,500 m³ cap.
